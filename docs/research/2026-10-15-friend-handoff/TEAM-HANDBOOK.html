Product and Decision Handoff

Version 1.0 · 15 October 2026 · US–Thailand P2P payments

1. Executive briefing

The project explores a consumer payment service in which a person chooses another person, sees a transparent quote, pays in ordinary money, and the recipient receives ordinary money in a bank account. Stablecoin settlement connects the service's partners behind the scenes. The user experience should not require an exchange account, seed phrase, blockchain wallet connection or gas management.

The long-term corridor is USD ↔ THB between the US and Thailand. The recommended hackathon demonstration starts with US → Thailand and uses simulated fiat operations around an actual testnet token transfer. Avalanche is confirmed by the owner; Fuji C-Chain and test USDC are the recommended implementation choices.

The hackathon has Web3 and AI tracks according to the owner. The current concept fits the Web3 track because blockchain settlement is its core technical element. AI was explicitly deferred. The owner identified Avalanche, Tokenomist and ElevenLabs as sponsors and confirmed that the team can choose relevant sponsors. Only Avalanche is recommended for the initial MVP. Prize rules have not been independently reviewed.

The central uncertainty is commercial and regulatory execution: whether the team can obtain approved fiat collection, stablecoin settlement and bank payout for general P2P customers on both sides at an attractive all-in cost. A functional testnet demo cannot resolve that uncertainty by itself.

4. Product definition

4.1 Intended customer journey

  1. Sign in to the application.
  2. Select or add a recipient in the supported destination country.
  3. Enter the source amount and permitted transfer purpose.
  4. Review source debit, exchange rate, fees, destination amount and delivery estimate.
  5. Confirm the quote and fund that transfer.
  6. Track funding, settlement and payout as separate milestones.
  7. Receive a receipt that identifies what actually happened.

The proposed demo uses an existing fictional recipient. It need not implement a two-sided social network, contact discovery, username search or recipient onboarding invitation to prove settlement.

“P2P” here means person-to-person remittance. It does not mean a decentralized marketplace matching strangers who manually trade crypto. The prototype uses a centrally operated backend even though settlement is on a public blockchain.

4.3 Positioning hypothesis

Potential value is a simple, transparent transfer experience with a known net recipient amount and reliable status. Small, repeated transfers are a useful research segment because fixed fees can matter disproportionately. This is a hypothesis to test, not a validated niche, pricing advantage or market size estimate.

Do not treat “walletless,” “uses stablecoins,” or “available in Thailand” as a unique moat. Existing remittance services already provide easy fiat experiences and local payout routes. Details are in RESEARCH-AND-LEGAL.md.

7. What “on chain” means here

The token transfer and its receipt are on Avalanche. The web app, user records, bank details, quotes and simulated payout stay off chain. Deploying a smart contract means publishing executable contract code to a blockchain and obtaining its address; it does not deploy the entire website or replace its database.

Calling the existing USDC contract is already an on-chain action. A custom contract may make transfer-specific rules publicly inspectable, but must have a concrete job. It does not establish real-world identity, approve money transmission or independently know whether a Thai bank credited a recipient.

Cross-border describes the real-world customer corridor. It does not imply cross-chain. Both settlement wallets can use the same Avalanche C-Chain; no bridge is required for this demo.

7. Monetary rules and quote example

Use integer minor units for fiat: USD cents and THB satang. Use integer token base units for USDC. JSON amounts are decimal strings so callers cannot lose precision through JavaScript number conversion. Reject negative, zero, malformed, over-limit and excessively precise input. Do not calculate money with binary floating point.

Represent an FX rate as a rational numerator/denominator or an exact decimal parsed by an appropriate decimal library. Specify the rounding rule. Proposed fixture rule: round destination minor units down; disclose this rule in the internal calculation record. Do not silently change rounding between quote and payout.

Illustrative example only, not a market price or promise:

Field Example
Sender's total debit USD 10.00 = 1000 cents
Explicit fee included in debit USD 0.20 = 20 cents
Amount converted USD 9.80 = 980 cents
Fixture rate 35 THB per USD
Net payout THB 343.00 = 34300 satang
Test settlement amount 9.8 test USDC = 9800000 base units

The example assumes a 1:1 USD-to-test-USDC accounting mapping only for the simulation. No test token is backed by the sender's simulated fiat, and no real redemption is performed. Additional FX spread, funding fees or payout fees are not silently assumed to be zero in a production cost model.

8. Proposed data model

All identifiers below are application identifiers; UUIDs are a practical default. Use UTC timestamps in storage and localize display. Record environment/mode on every transfer.

Entity Essential fields Important constraints
profiles auth user ID, display name, demo country Auth identity is not KYC proof
recipients ID, owner ID, fictional name, country, currency, bank fixture Owner-scoped; no real bank data in demo
quotes ID, owner ID, recipient snapshot, immutable amounts, rate, expiry, accepted time One accepted transfer per quote; supported corridor only
transfers ID, owner ID, quote ID, idempotency key, request fingerprint, status, version, mode Unique owner+idempotency key; unique quote ID
funding_attempts ID, transfer ID, provider request ID, simulated status, reason Unique logical funding operation; safe replay
settlement_attempts ID, transfer ID, chain ID, token, signer, destination, units, nonce, signed tx, hash, receipt block, outcome Unique chain+signer+nonce and chain+hash; serialize unresolved signer operations
payout_attempts ID, transfer ID, request ID, currency, net amount, simulated status, reference Stable payout request ID across unknown-outcome retries
transfer_events ID, transfer ID, sequence, previous/new status, reason, attempt reference, time Append-only; unique transfer+sequence
jobs ID, type, transfer ID, dedupe key, status, lease, attempts, next-run time Unique logical dedupe key; exclusive claim

The database stores a history of attempts. Never overwrite an uncertain settlement attempt with a new hash and lose the ability to reconcile it. An append-only event history is an audit aid, not a complete financial ledger. Production would require a designed double-entry ledger, reconciliation controls and appropriate handling of customer liabilities even without a stored-balance UX.

Use database transactions for each state change together with its event and next job. This is a small transactional outbox pattern: a crash must not leave a successful state change without its required follow-up job. Enforce valid transitions in the service/domain layer and use database uniqueness to defend against concurrent requests.

Expected transfer creation request:

  "quoteId": "quote_demo_001",
  "purpose": "PERSONAL_REMITTANCE"
}

The source amount, recipient and settlement destination are resolved from server-owned records, not accepted as override fields on this call. A future workflow may require a recipient revision; that should create a new quote rather than mutate an accepted transfer.

Idempotency behavior:

  • Same authenticated owner, same key, same payload: return the original transfer and current status.
  • Same owner and key, different payload: 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT.
  • Same quote with another key after acceptance: return a documented conflict rather than create a second transfer.
  • Persist the idempotency reservation with creation in one database transaction.

Suggested errors: 400 INVALID_INPUT, 401 UNAUTHENTICATED, 404 NOT_FOUND, 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT, 409 INVALID_STATE, 410 QUOTE_EXPIRED, 422 UNSUPPORTED_CORRIDOR, 429 RATE_LIMITED, 503 SETTLEMENT_UNAVAILABLE. Do not leak another account's transfer existence. Authenticated mutations need CSRF protection appropriate to the selected session transport; demo controls must not rely on a visible UI button being hidden.

No production provider webhook is implemented by this proposal. When a real provider is later integrated, add signature verification, replay protection, event deduplication, legal-data handling and reconciliation according to that provider's actual contract.

10. State machine

Quote lifecycle is separate: OPEN → ACCEPTED or EXPIRED. A quote is not a funded transfer.

Pre-broadcast setup problems such as insufficient test gas are recorded as blocked jobs with a reason while preserving the funded transfer. They are not fabricated transaction reverts. A timeout alone is not SETTLEMENT_FAILED. REVIEW_REQUIRED is a durable hold with retained evidence; it does not automatically refund, retry or mark success.

An explicit operator action may recover a reviewed or known-failed transfer after evidence is checked. That path must not be a generic “force success” endpoint. A new settlement attempt is allowed only after the previous attempt is conclusively unable to pay, under an intentionally designed retry policy. Automatic new-nonce retries are outside the initial baseline.

11. Settlement algorithm and failure recovery

11.1 Prepare and submit

  1. Claim a due settlement job using a database lease and lock the transfer.
  2. Confirm funded-simulated status and that no verified or unresolved prior settlement exists.
  3. Hold exclusive access to the service signer. The minimal demo can process only one unresolved transaction at a time per signer; this sacrifices throughput for understandable nonce safety.
  4. Validate chain ID, token address, destination allowlist, amount, balances and gas configuration. Verify token decimals on chain rather than trusting a label.
  5. Reserve the nonce, build and sign the ERC-20 transfer, compute its hash locally, and persist the signed transaction and attempt before sending it.
  6. Broadcast the exact stored bytes. A response saying “already known” or a network timeout does not authorize a different payment.
  7. Continue receipt reconciliation using the stored hash. Renew the job lease or let another worker resume from the persisted attempt.

Do not send the signed transaction before the persistence transaction commits. Signed raw bytes are not the private key, but they are executable authorization to move test tokens and should be restricted to the worker, excluded from logs and retained only as needed for reconciliation.

11.2 Verify outcome

Check the expected network, transaction hash, receipt success, token contract address, ERC-20 Transfer event, sender wallet, destination wallet and exact base-unit amount. A receipt with a successful status but no matching token event is not sufficient proof of the intended settlement.

A proposed conservative demo setting is two block confirmations followed by a fresh canonical-block/receipt check. This is an application policy, not a claim that Avalanche requires two confirmations. Pin and test the policy against the actual RPC and network behavior before the demonstration. If contradictory reads occur, hold payout and reconcile.

Store receipt block number/hash, observed token event and verification time. Mark SETTLED and enqueue the unique payout job in the same database transaction.

11.3 Recover safely

Situation Required behavior
User double-clicks confirm Return the same transfer
Worker crashes before signing Resume from job; no chain side effect occurred
Worker crashes after signed attempt is stored Reconcile hash; rebroadcast same bytes if appropriate
RPC accepted broadcast but response was lost Query stored hash; never invent a new payment
Same nonce appears consumed by an unknown transaction Hold for review and inspect signer activity
Transaction reverted Preserve failure receipt; do not pay fiat or mark tokens delivered
Chain transaction succeeds but database write fails Reconcile that hash again and persist the single settlement result
Payout succeeds but response/database update is lost Reconcile the stable payout request ID; do not blindly pay again
Payout is known failed after chain settlement Retry only payout with the defined adapter policy
UI closes Worker continues; returning UI reads persisted status

Leases alone do not guarantee exactly-once money movement. Safety comes from persisted signed attempts, signer serialization, unique constraints, state guards and outcome reconciliation. A direct ERC-20 transfer has no application transfer-ID deduplication built into the token contract.

15. Optional custom contract

If event rules require a team-deployed contract, consider a small settlement router with an opaque unique transfer ID, an allowlisted token and destination policy, an authorized backend operator, duplicate-ID rejection, and a settlement event. Transfer tokens through SafeERC20-style checked operations. Record completion only when the token transfer succeeds atomically.

Use Solidity plus an established contract library and a tested deployment toolchain selected at implementation time. Contract tests must cover duplicate IDs, unauthorized callers, disallowed tokens/destinations, failed token transfer rollback and exact amounts. Do not introduce arbitrary admin withdrawal, long-lived escrow, upgradeability, public remittance claiming or fee custody without a clear requirement and review.

This extension changes the baseline and should be an explicit team decision. It can bind an on-chain settlement to an application identifier, but cannot prove KYC, funds ownership or fiat payout. Avoid storing even hashed personal details on chain; use random opaque IDs.

First target: a local Next.js server plus a continuously running worker, both using the same database. A laptop presentation needs reliable internet for Supabase and Fuji RPC. Public deployment should be selected only after confirming event needs.

Run the worker as a supervised service or implement a deliberately designed durable job runner. Database-backed jobs must persist across web deployment restarts.

3. Why the method might cost less

Traditional correspondent transfers can involve several intermediaries, separate reconciliations, banking cutoffs and prefunded balances. A stablecoin settlement leg may reduce some of these steps, permit continuous settlement and change working-capital requirements. SCB's Lightnet announcement describes a stablecoin-backed cross-border project that graduated from a Bank of Thailand sandbox. It is a precedent for the architecture, not approval for our team or proof of Avalanche support.

The proposed method adds or retains fiat collection costs, stablecoin acquisition/redemption, FX spread, blockchain gas, local payout charges, KYC/AML operations, fraud losses, reserves, chargebacks, support and liquidity. Destination THB may still need to be prefunded even if stablecoins move quickly.

The cost-saving condition is:

Avoided intermediary, reconciliation and capital costs
    > Added conversion, network, partner and risk costs

This is a hypothesis about a particular route and funding method. Modern remittance companies also use local collection/payout and efficient treasury systems; comparing only with an expensive SWIFT wire overstates the opportunity. Small gas fees do not establish a low total price, and testnet gas cannot be used as a production fee quote.

Research references from the preceding discussion include the Federal Reserve's cross-border stablecoin analysis, BIS CPMI analysis, and a Banca d'Italia study. These were discussed earlier; the particular studies are not a measurement of this team's US–Thailand program and were not re-audited in full while packaging this handoff.

4. Cost comparison method

For USD→THB define:

  • D: total USD debited from sender, including charges paid outside the app if applicable.
  • R: THB actually received net of receiving-side charges.
  • M: contemporaneous reference mid-market THB per USD.
All-in cost (%) = 100 × [1 − R / (D × M)]

For THB→USD use total THB debit, net USD receipt and the matching USD-per-THB reference rate. Do not invert one provider's retail rate and call it the reference rate. Keep timing, funding method, recipient route and transfer purpose comparable.

Illustrative arithmetic: if the total debit is USD 10, reference rate is 35 THB/USD and net receipt is THB 343, the all-in cost is 2%. These numbers are invented to explain measurement; they are not our actual fee schedule or a competitor quote.

Collect quotes at USD 10, 25, 50, 100 and 1,000 equivalents. Separate first-transfer promotions from repeat-user prices. A fixed USD 1.99 fee alone is 19.9% of USD 10 and 1.99% of USD 100, but that arithmetic says nothing about additional FX or receiving charges.

Cost/quote field Capture
Time and eligibility Timestamp/timezone, sender country/address/account type and applicable state
Funding Bank/card method, card type, provider fee and external issuer/bank charges
FX Reference rate/time, provider rate, spread, quote expiry and guarantee
Settlement Token acquisition fee/spread, chain fee, redemption/off-ramp cost
Payout Bank/card/cash route, partner charge, receiving deductions and currency
Outcome Total source debit, final net receipt, estimated and observed delivery time
Commercial assumptions Partner minimums, reserves, monthly fees, prefunding, failures and refunds

6. Legal perimeter: Thailand

The Bank of Thailand payment FAQ, question 4.5 describes payment-transfer licensing and international money-transfer-agent permission under the payment-systems and exchange-control frameworks. Questions 4.6 and 4.8 also address foreign providers and KYC. This establishes that international transfers have a regulatory perimeter; it does not decide the exact permissions for the team's unselected contractual role.

Review separately: domestic collection, international transfer, FX, control/holding of stablecoins, digital-asset conversion, payout, consumer communications and personal-data handling. Thai outbound treatment deserves specific review; hiding a stablecoin leg in the backend is not an exemption.

The earlier review also flagged BOT foreign-exchange guidance. Its application to the exact funds/title flow needs qualified review; no full legal interpretation of that PDF is asserted here.

The SEC release of 11 September 2026 concerns a consultation on proposed stablecoin supervision principles and states a hearing end date of 25 September 2026. Expiry of that comment period is not proof of enactment. This package has not established a subsequent final rule or effective date. Verify final publications before saying the proposed rules apply or do not apply to the production model.

7. Legal perimeter: United States

FinCEN's virtual-currency guidance treats certain acceptance/transmission or exchange activities as money transmission depending on facts, circumstances and applicable exclusions. A hidden stablecoin leg does not by itself take the activity outside that analysis. Federal MSB issues are distinct from state licensing, authorized-delegate arrangements and other applicable obligations.

The Texas Department of Banking MSB FAQ, discussed previously, is a useful state-specific reference for delegate scope. It is not a nationwide licensing conclusion. Determine the actual sender states and contracting entities before relying on a partner arrangement.

The CFPB remittance resource page points to rules on disclosures, estimates, error resolution, cancellation/refunds and agents. Applicability, exceptions and current amendments need review for the approved program. The testnet receipt design is not a claim that statutory disclosures are satisfied.

A regulated partner's contract may allocate tasks while leaving duties with the platform. Obtain explicit agreement on who contracts with the sender, delivers disclosures, handles complaints, refunds and fraud, and bears losses. API access is not an umbrella license.

8. Payment processor restrictions

Stripe's restricted-business policy lists peer-to-peer money transmission among prohibited financial activities. A standard merchant checkout is therefore not the assumed collection rail for this product. Approval for a creator/content payment model would not automatically cover general personal remittance.

Card-funded remittance programs can exist through appropriate acquiring/network arrangements; that does not mean ordinary merchant processing qualifies. The earlier review identified Visa Direct documentation as a starting point for funding/push-payment structures. Actual program sponsorship, jurisdiction, use case and responsibilities remain unconfirmed.

For the hackathon use a mock funding adapter, not an ordinary live checkout disguised as a transfer. No card numbers or bank authentication should be collected by this prototype.
